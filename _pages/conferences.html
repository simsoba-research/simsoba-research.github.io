  • Deep Learning Indaba 20262 to 7 Aug 2026
    Pan-Atlantic University, Lagos, Nigeria · Theme: Sovereign Intelligence: Africa's Path in a Frontier AI World

    The Indaba is the largest yearly gathering of the African machine learning community. It was founded in 2017, is now a registered charity run by volunteers, and admits delegates by application. The 2026 edition was the eighth and welcomed more than 1,000 people from about 45 countries. Its theme asks how Africa can build, own and understand its own AI systems, data and science. Google DeepMind and Google Research were among the sponsors, and the opening keynote on Wednesday 5 August, by Prof. Vukosi Marivate, asked what benchmarks for African-language AI actually measure.

    I followed the sessions on computer vision, trustworthy and ethical AI design, agentic AI, and AI evaluation in African contexts.

  • MMED Clinic: Meaningful Modelling of Epidemiological Data15 to 26 Jun 2026
    ICI3D programme, SACEMA (Stellenbosch University) · AIMS South Africa, Muizenberg

    MMED is a two-week clinic on data-driven, dynamical models of disease spread, now in its 15th edition. Seminars, tutorials and individual mentoring prepare each participant to complete a team modelling project on real data. The ICI3D clinics have trained hundreds of researchers in sub-Saharan Africa since 2009, and in 2026 the programme received a €1.3 million EDCTP3 grant to expand its training hubs.

    With my team I modelled Mpox transmission in South Kivu, DRC, fitting SIR, SEIR and stochastic SEIRV models, with R-based data cleaning and a final presentation.

  • Deep Learning Indaba 202517 to 22 Aug 2025
    University of Rwanda, Kigali · Theme: Urunana, Hand in Hand for AI in Africa

    The seventh Indaba drew more than 1,000 researchers, students, startups and policymakers. Keynotes came from Verena Rieser (Google DeepMind), Max Welling (University of Amsterdam), Dina Machuve (DevData Analytics) and Sanmi Koyejo (Stanford), and a flagship panel discussed responsible, inclusive AI policy for Africa. The week also included a two-day African Research Symposium, tutorials on machine learning foundations, reinforcement learning, NLP and the attention mechanism, and more than 200 posters.

    I attended the workshops on NLP, reinforcement learning, AI in healthcare and AI governance, and the sessions on machine learning at the edge.
