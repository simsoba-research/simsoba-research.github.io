Course Syllabus · UE MVA003

72 htotal volume 6ECTS credits LP157first-year professional bachelor's (Licence professionnelle) ESAMLomé, Togo

The course gives software-engineering students the maths behind everyday computing: sizing a keyspace, estimating how reliable a system is, ordering dependent tasks, working with ciphers, designing a logic circuit and proving an algorithm correct.

  • Set Theory

    Sets, subsets and operations; Venn diagrams; power sets and Cartesian products; cardinality and inclusion-exclusion.

    Applied to: Data modelling and set-based queries.

  • Combinatorics

    Rules of sum and product; permutations and combinations; binomial coefficients; the pigeonhole principle.

    Applied to: Keyspace counting: how many passwords, keys or identifiers a policy allows.

  • Probability

    Sample spaces and events; conditional probability and independence; Bayes' rule.

    Applied to: System reliability: failure probabilities of series and parallel components.

  • Relations

    Binary relations and their properties; equivalence relations and classes; partial orders and Hasse diagrams.

    Applied to: Task-dependency ordering, as in build systems and scheduling.

  • Arithmetic

    Divisibility; the Euclidean algorithm and gcd; primes; modular arithmetic and inverses.

    Applied to: Cryptographic arithmetic, up to RSA.

  • Boolean Algebra

    Boolean functions and laws; truth tables; normal forms; simplification.

    Applied to: Digital-circuit design with logic gates.

  • Logic and Induction

    Propositional and predicate logic; quantifiers; proof techniques; mathematical induction.

    Applied to: Algorithmic induction: proving loops and recursive algorithms correct.

What Students Take Away
  • Count configurations rigorously, to size keyspaces and search spaces.
  • Quantify reliability and risk with probability.
  • Reason about order and dependency with relations and partial orders.
  • Use modular arithmetic, Boolean algebra and induction as working tools in cryptography, hardware and algorithm design.
